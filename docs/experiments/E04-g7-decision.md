# E04 — G7's colour: red stays. Delegated to the executor, decided on the work.

**The Director, 2026-08-04:** *"I'm cool either way on the color of the cannons. Your choice."*

He is neutral on the look, so this is decided on what serves the pipeline rather than on
taste. **G7 stays `red-lined gun port lids`, exactly as authored.** The lids that arrived warm
brown stay a recorded miss.

## Why keeping it is worth more than amending it

Amending would make the canon describe the artifact — tidy, and worth nothing. Keeping it
gives E04 a **known-miss element with a named mechanism**, and the mechanism is not a guess.

**G7 is the only one of the twelve whose colour term modifies a sub-feature instead of its
head noun — and it is the only one that did not land.**

| | elements | not landed |
|---|---|---|
| colour occupies the **head noun** (*gilded spire*, *black barrels*, *tan sails*…) | 11 | **0** |
| colour occupies a **sub-feature** of an already-occupied surface (*red-**lined** lids*) | 1 | **1** |

Every other element states a colour for the whole object. `red-lined` asks for a red *lining*
on a lid whose surface is already occupied by hull material.

**This is the second instance of a mechanism this repo already measured**, and the first on a
non-character subject. From `CLAUDE.md`, earned on W3:

> **Specify from scratch; never patch.** A specification determines what *occupies* a surface
> and cannot add a second element to one already occupied. Asking for a gold plate onto an
> existing fur cuff produced **no response at all** — ΔE 1.07, in two different grammatical
> forms, where elements that *replaced* their surface's occupant landed in full.

G7 is that failure again: an additive element onto an occupied surface, and it produced no red
anywhere on the ship. The fixture's own Form section cites Amendment 15 and predicts exactly
this — *"nothing is an addition onto an occupied surface, and any future edit that becomes one
is predicted to drop."* **G7 was already an addition when it was written, and nobody noticed,
including me when I authored the prompt from it.**

## The bound on this claim

**n = 1 on this subject.** The separation is clean (1 of 1 against 0 of 11) and it matches a
mechanism measured independently on W3, but a single instance is a corroboration, not a
finding. It is offered to the E04 spec as a **pre-registered, falsifiable test**, not as an
established result.

## The testable prediction it buys

If the mechanism is the additive grammar, then restating G7 so the colour occupies the head
noun — **`red gun port lids`** rather than `red-lined gun port lids`, one word removed —
should make it land, with nothing else changed. If it lands, the mechanism is confirmed on a
second subject and the grammar rule earns a ship-side instance. If it still misses, the cause
is elsewhere (size, occlusion, or the LoRA's warm register pulling it) and that is worth
knowing too.

**Either outcome is informative, which is the whole reason to keep the miss.** Amending to
brown would have thrown away a free experiment to make a document tidier.
